High-risk merchant account UK guide: how to present pre-orders to an acquirer

Prepare a high-risk merchant account UK application for pre-orders. Calculate undelivered sales, document dispatch dates and compare reserve terms.

AQ Pay Payments Team·15 November 2026·5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Show how much customers have paid for orders still awaiting delivery on a particular date.
  • Keep overdue orders visible even when a supplier gives you a new dispatch date.
  • Compare a proposed reserve and its release schedule against the cash needed to fulfil outstanding orders.

A UK retailer can collect £24,000 on Monday and still owe customers £24,000 worth of goods on Friday. A bank statement shows the receipts. It takes an order ledger to show what customers are waiting for. If your search for “high-risk merchant account UK” started with a planned pre-order launch, bring both views to your first conversation about high-risk payment processing.

The question for the acquirer is specific: can it support this product, sold by this company, with payment collected this far ahead of delivery? A retailer selling from stock and the same retailer taking full payment for next season’s production run can present quite different delivery risks. Here is how to explain the difference and compare the answers you receive.

High-risk merchant account UK applications for pre-orders

Start with one real product. Write down the promise on the product page, when you collect payment, when the supplier commits stock and what counts as completed delivery. Mark the point where a customer can reach someone about an overdue order. A screenshot of a checkout button explains very little without the dates on either side of it.

There is a reason to discuss that interval. Visa’s guidance for payment facilitators and marketplaces identifies delayed delivery as one circumstance in which a reserve may be appropriate for a sponsored merchant. The guidance does not prescribe a universal reserve percentage. Ask the prospective acquirer to assess your actual business and explain the terms it proposes. Visa’s merchant reserve guidance, section 8.6

If the application is your first, use the merchant underwriting guide for the wider business and ownership documents. The additional exhibit for pre-orders is a dated account of the outstanding delivery promise.

Work out the value still waiting for delivery

Illustrative example. A furniture retailer has collected £200 in full for each of 150 stools. Each order contains one stool. At Friday’s cut-off, 30 customers have received theirs. The remaining 120 paid orders represent £24,000 of goods still to deliver. This example assumes no cancellations, partial orders or refunds. It measures customer commitments in pounds; it is neither a loss forecast nor a proposed reserve.

Promised delivery Paid orders still open Amount collected on open orders What to show
Next week 80 £16,000 Allocated stock and booked carrier collection
Following week 40 £8,000 Supplier confirmation and expected arrival
Total at Friday close 120 £24,000 A dated extract that reconciles to the two rows

Now suppose the second batch slips by a fortnight. The total outstanding value is initially identical, but those 40 customers will wait longer. Preserve the original promised date alongside the revised date. If you overwrite it, next Friday’s extract can make late orders look on time. Attach the supplier update, the message sent to affected customers and a count of customers who requested refunds.

For a business that ships in parts, do the calculation by order line. For a deposit, record only the money already collected in the paid column. Keep a separate column for refunds requested but still unpaid. Otherwise an order cancelled in the shop system can disappear from the open list while the customer is still waiting for money.

Make the customer promise easy to verify

Walk through the mobile checkout as a first-time buyer. Can you see that the item is a pre-order before paying? Does the confirmation repeat the same delivery window? If the product page says “delivery in June” and the email says “dispatch in June”, your team has made two different promises. Save dated screenshots of both and agree which wording operations can support.

For UK distance sales, government guidance lists information that sellers must provide before an order, including delivery arrangements, costs and timing, and applicable cancellation information. Check the guidance and any exceptions for your particular goods with your adviser. Give the reviewer the customer-facing wording as well as the internal procedure for handling a missed date. GOV.UK guidance on distance selling

Take one anonymised late order through customer support. Identify who noticed the delay, when the buyer heard about it and where a refund request would appear in the accounts. If the refund has been issued, show the reference that connects it to the original payment. Remove names, addresses and card details from the sample you share.

Compare two offers against the same delivery week

Ask each provider for the legal name of the acquiring or payment services firm, the party making the underwriting decision and the party sending settlements. The FCA advises checking the firm behind the trading name and its permissions on the Financial Services Register. Use the legal name from the proposed agreement when making that check. FCA guidance on checking payment providers

Give both providers the Friday ledger and ask them to explain a week of settlement in writing. Which receipts count towards any reserve? When would each withheld amount become eligible for release? Can a delivery delay trigger a review? Who can your finance team contact if an expected release is missing? Record any unanswered question beside the relevant clause before comparing prices.

For example, if one illustrative offer withheld 10% of £30,000 of eligible receipts, that deduction alone would be £3,000. It would not tell you the amount in your next payout: fees, refunds, settlement timing and earlier reserve releases would also matter. Put the proposed payout date beside the date the warehouse needs paying. A dispatch plan that depends on cash arriving earlier than the contract allows needs revisiting before launch.

Bring a decision your team can act on

  1. Have finance date and reconcile the open-order extract. Give operations a copy of the same snapshot so both teams can explain the totals.
  2. Ask fulfilment to identify the oldest overdue order and the largest group dependent on one supplier. Record the action owner and next update date for each.
  3. Ask support to demonstrate one cancellation from customer request to payment confirmation. Document any manual hand-off and who checks that it finishes.
  4. Have the acquirer confirm whether the proposed product, collection timing and maximum delivery window fall within its offer. Ask what changes would need a fresh review.

Keep that record after launch. If you move from deposits to full prepayment, sell a larger batch or extend the delivery window, the original example no longer describes the business. Prepare an updated snapshot for the next discussion.

Frequently asked questions

Does accepting pre-orders automatically make a UK merchant high risk?

An individual acquirer decides whether your model fits its appetite. Describe what customers pay upfront, how long delivery takes and what happens if a supplier misses a deadline.

Should I include the full selling price if customers only pay a deposit?

Show amounts actually collected and still outstanding separately from the remaining order value. Explain when the balance becomes payable so the reviewer can distinguish cash already received from expected future receipts.

What can a new business show without processing history?

Label projections as forecasts. Provide the assumptions behind the first batch, supplier commitments, a sample checkout and a dated plan for comparing actual deliveries with the forecast.
